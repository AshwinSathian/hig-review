# Slop catalog

catalog-date: 2026-10-23
review-by: 2027-04-23

This is our list of convergence tells: the defaults, library looks, trend looks and copied praise that make an interface read as assembled rather than authored. It is the canonical, de-duplicated home for every "Slop tells (ours)" line in references/apple/*.md and references/augment/*.md, the convergence catalog from research/digests/13, the overused-pattern notes from digests 09 to 12 and 14, and the AI-microcopy tables from digest 15. Every entry is ours and carries the Taste tag unless the Source column names an outside source; none of it is an Apple rule, and a sourced entry is only as strong as its source.

One tell proves little, because single patterns are often legitimate, so the signal is a cluster (thresholds under Counting below). A pattern is fine when its validity condition holds, such as a skeleton on a full-page load of 1 to 10 s, a carousel the person drives, or a sidebar on iPad, and platform conventions (tab bars, navigation stacks, sidebars on iPad and Mac, standard controls) are never tells by themselves.

The catalog also guards the skill's own output: before any Elevate move or distinctiveness direction is written, it is checked against every section here, including praised-product clones and second-layer defaults, and a match is dropped rather than softened.

How to use it:
- **Channels** for the cluster count: accent color, neutrals, typeface and type treatment, shape and radius, icon style, illustration and imagery, motion, sound and haptics, copy voice, layout idiom, signature interaction (digest 13, distinctiveness pass step 2).
- **Counting.** One row is one tell; a row that appears in two sections counts once, and each tell lives in one best section with cross-references elsewhere. Two thresholds, both ours and not published figures: (1) the distinctiveness procedure (references/augment/distinctiveness.md step 10) sets the verdict, and this cluster threshold (tells across 3 or more channels, or 5 or more tells in total) is an input that forces "default-assembled" when met; (2) a single finding is raised when 3 or more tells sit in one section, or on any praised-product clone signature (digest 13 step 8). Pixelslop's bands (CLEAN 0-1, MILD 2-3, SLOPPY 4-6, TERMINAL 7+, pixelslop@0.3.8, digest 13 §A6) are reference only and set no verdict here. Its test is the one to keep: did a person make this choice, or did the generator fall back to a default?
- **Detection.** Where `scripts/measure.js` reports a signal, the row names it exactly: an entry in `defaults` by its `tell` string, or a field of `motion`, `type`, `contrast` or `targets`. Otherwise the row says what to look for in code or a screenshot.
- **Better pattern** columns state a principle or a decision process. They never name a font, palette, library or product look as the fix, and they never fix slop by substitution (one sans for another, indigo for terracotta, one icon set for another): the replacement is usually already listed under second-layer defaults.
- **Guardrail for our own suggestions.** Each suggestion cites its source in this product (subject, user, content, an existing asset or a stated principle); "looks premium" or "looks modern" is not a source. Ask "would this fit any product?" and rewrite or drop it if yes. A finding phrased as a generic principle name with no screen-specific evidence is itself a tell (references/apple/principles-platforms.md).
- **Source key.** Two-digit numbers are research/digests/NN. "Taste (Apple refs)" marks our tells gathered from the "Slop tells (ours)" lines in references/apple/*.md. HW = the humanizing-writing skill; Atl = Atlassian; MC = Mailchimp; NNg = Nielsen Norman Group; MS = Microsoft; "spec" = docs/specs/2026-10-23-hig-review-design.md.

## AI-microcopy

Dated with this catalog because copy trends move faster than rules; recheck it when new model releases or design-system updates change the patterns. Behind every row is the pattern humanizing-writing names: copy reads as machine-written when every choice in it would suit any product and any user. **Robotic** copy (2010s-era template text) and **trending-generic** copy (the 2020s "friendly startup" voice models learned from it) are equally interchangeable. These are review flags, not banned words: "simply" is fine in "Simply Piano" (a name), and "unlock" is right for a door lock. Count tells density per screen; one hit proves little and a cluster is the signal. A last judgment test for any string: swap in a competitor's name, and if nothing breaks, it is generic. Rules and rewrites for each message type live in references/writing.md.
